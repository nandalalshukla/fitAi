# Content research

How the FormFit plans and exercise guides were designed. Principles are summarised in our own
words (no copied text); where evidence is mixed we chose the conservative option and say so.
Research was done with web search in October 2026. Several primary sources were only reachable
through abstracts and reputable secondary summaries; those are marked _(summary)_.

## Key principles → design decisions

| Principle (paraphrased)                                                                                                                                       | Source   | How it shapes the plans                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adults should do muscle-strengthening for all major muscle groups on ≥2 days a week, plus 150–300 min moderate (or 75–150 min vigorous) aerobic activity.     | S3, S1   | Every plan has ≥2 strength days (beginner plans 3). Express/HIIT plans note that daily walking completes the aerobic target.                                                                             |
| Growth follows a dose–response with weekly sets; ~10+ hard sets per muscle per week is a practical target for hypertrophy.                                    | S4, S1   | _Bodyweight Muscle Builder_ gives chest, shoulders, quads and glutes ≥10 sets a week across two sessions; other plans sit lower on purpose (general fitness).                                            |
| Light loads taken close to failure build muscle similarly to heavy loads; strength gains favour heavier loading.                                              | S5       | Bodyweight is a light load, so muscle plans use higher reps (12–20) and harder variations instead of more weight; strength plans progress to harder leverage (incline → knee → full → decline push-ups). |
| Training closer to failure gives a small extra growth benefit, but training to failure isn't required and adds fatigue.                                       | S6, S1   | Sets are meant to end 1–3 reps short of failure (stated in _what to expect_); volume rises slowly rather than every set to failure.                                                                      |
| Rests above ~60–90 s give slightly better growth; beyond ~90 s there is little extra. Short rests suit endurance/conditioning.                                | S7       | Strength/muscle plans rest 60–90 s; conditioning plans 20–45 s, with rests shortening a little each week.                                                                                                |
| Novices: 2–3 days/week, moderate reps; progress when the current target is easy for 1–2 extra reps (~2–10 % increments).                                      | S2       | Volume grows 5–10 % per week (`volumePerWeek`), harder variations unlock after 2–4 weeks (`progressTo`).                                                                                                 |
| Periodic lighter weeks reduce accumulated fatigue without losing gains.                                                                                       | S8       | Plans of 6+ weeks with high frequency deload in week 4 (and week 8 in the advanced plan) at 60–65 % volume, one fewer set.                                                                               |
| Dynamic warm-ups (~5–10 min) prepare for explosive work; long static stretching right before training can blunt performance.                                  | S10      | All sessions start with 4–6 min of dynamic moves (marching, arm/hip circles, jacks or hinges); static holds only in cool-downs and the mobility plan.                                                    |
| Flexibility: ≥2–3 days/week, holds ~10–30 s (longer for older adults), ~60 s total per muscle.                                                                | S9       | Cool-down holds are 30–60 s; the mobility plan does 2 × 30 s per stretch, growing ~10 %/week.                                                                                                            |
| You can't “spot reduce” belly fat with ab exercise; visible abs depend on overall body-fat level (nutrition, sleep, activity).                                | S11      | The 6-Pack plan says so plainly, trains the whole body for energy expenditure, and frames ab work as strength and endurance.                                                                             |
| Spine-sparing core work (curl-up, side bridge, bird dog) builds stability with low lumbar load; repeated loaded flexion is less friendly for sensitive backs. | S12      | Core & Posture and Low-Impact plans use the McGill big three; flexion moves (sit-ups, crunches) are limited and the coach review replaced daily sit-ups with bird dogs.                                  |
| Letting the knees travel forward in a squat is normal; blocking it shifts stress to the hips and lower back.                                                  | S13      | Squat cues say “knees track over toes” rather than “never past toes”. The lunge cue fires only on _excessive_ travel (balance/step length), not as a safety alarm.                                       |
| Interval work:rest ratios: ~1:2 for beginners moving towards 1:1 or 2:1 as fitness improves.                                                                  | S14      | Fat Burn starts near 1:1 (30 s work / 30 s rest) and shortens rest weekly (`restPerWeekS`); jumping work has low-impact alternatives.                                                                    |
| Raising the hands (incline) makes push-ups easier; raising the feet (decline) makes them harder and more shoulder-dominant.                                   | S15      | Push-up ladder: incline → knee → full → decline, with the alternatives listed on every exercise.                                                                                                         |
| Screen for red flags (heart symptoms, chest pain, dizziness, joint problems, pregnancy) before increasing activity.                                           | S16, S17 | Onboarding safety screen + every plan's safety notes; pregnancy/postpartum and high-impact cautions on relevant exercises.                                                                               |
| Exercise in many forms is first-line for non-specific low-back pain, tailored to the person.                                                                  | S18      | Low-Impact Basics is explicitly _general fitness, not treatment_; it uses gentle, tailorable moves and tells users to follow their clinician.                                                            |

### Mixed or contested evidence — conservative choices

- **Volume above ~20 sets/muscle/week:** evidence leans towards more growth but with diminishing returns
  and more fatigue; we stay at ~10–15 sets.
- **Daily ab training:** fine for endurance at moderate volume, but loaded spinal flexion every day is
  debated; the 6-Pack plan alternates flexion (crunches, leg raises) with anti-extension/anti-rotation work
  and keeps one recovery and one rest day.
- **Static stretching and injury prevention:** no clear injury reduction; we use it for flexibility only.
- **Jumping (burpees, jump squats, skaters):** limited to intermediate/advanced plans, never in
  beginner/low-impact plans, always with a no-jump alternative.

## How plans are built (and tested)

Plans are authored as session templates + a weekly pattern + typed progression rules and compiled
to day-by-day schedules (`src/content/compile.ts`, ADR-028). Tests (`src/content/__tests__`) assert:
valid schema and exercise ids; every session warm-up → main → cool-down; a rest or active-recovery
day every week; volume increases and deload weeks are lighter; the big muscles (quads, glutes,
hamstrings, chest, triceps, shoulders, upper back) are not loaded with ≥6 hard sets on two
consecutive days; every exercise has easier and harder options and an animated demo; estimated
session length (work + rests + 15 s per exercise, tempo-aware) within ±15 % of the advertised minutes.

## Exercise library

54 exercises (11 camera-tracked). Each has: category, difficulty, primary/secondary muscles,
equipment, 4–5 numbered steps (setup → movement → end position, with breathing and where to look),
coach tips, common mistakes with fixes, easier and harder variations (linked when in the library),
safety notes and camera placement. Tracked exercises' form cues reuse the voice-cue keys so the
spoken and written cues match. Every exercise has an original procedurally-animated demo built from
keyframes in the pose-engine skeleton format (`src/features/exercises/animation/keyframes`), tested
for rigid limbs, anatomical joint ranges and loop perfection, and — for tracked exercises — to land
inside the rep counter's thresholds.

## Coach self-review (October 2026)

Re-read every plan as a strength coach; changes made:

1. **6-Pack Abs:** session A and B both loaded legs hard on consecutive days → rebuilt as upper/lower
   alternation (A keeps a light 2 × 20 squat block, matching the requested push-up/squat/jack session).
   Daily sit-ups replaced with bird dogs on B days to limit repeated loaded flexion.
2. **Full-Body Fat Burn:** B day repeated squats after A's squats/lunges → swapped for shoulder taps.
3. **Advanced Calisthenics:** conditioning day added squats right after leg day → replaced with
   bicycle crunches; high-knee volume kept below the consecutive-day threshold.
4. **Bodyweight Muscle Builder:** chest volume was ~7 sets/week (below the ~10-set target) → added
   tempo incline push-ups and a fourth push-up set.
5. **Beginner Foundations:** full push-ups in week 1 are unrealistic for many beginners → incline
   push-ups first, knee push-ups from week 3; crunches → McGill curl-ups.
6. **Session lengths:** several sessions were 20–30 % shorter than advertised (express, mobility,
   upper-body, advanced) → added sensible volume until all fall within ±15 %; tempo now counts
   towards session time.
7. **Known limitation:** without equipment, horizontal/vertical pulling is under-trained; plans use
   back extensions and Y raises and say that a band or pull-up bar would help.
